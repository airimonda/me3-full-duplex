  • Watson is running on the Pi: ssh watsonpi 'pgrep -fl pipecat_assistant' shows one process. If not: ssh watsonpi 'setsid nohup bash ~/me3/run_live.sh >/dev/null 2>&1 </dev/null &'

  • The Watson UI runs on the Pi: open http://192.168.1.80:8301 on the Mac (use the Pi's current IP). If it doesn't load: ssh watsonpi 'setsid nohup bash ~/me3/run_ui.sh >/dev/null 2>&1 </dev/null &'

  • Wait 2 minutes after any restart. The voice model loads for about 100 s, and speech recognition is slow until it finishes.
  • Say "Watson" once. You should hear "Yes? I'm listening." in the Deepgram Jupiter voice.
  • If Watson ignores you (log shows stranger_ignored), someone else holds the session: say "Watson, it's me again" to take it back. Only if that fails, restore your saved profile and restart Watson:

0:00 · 30 s Opening

Most voice assistants are walkie-talkies: you talk, they talk, and nobody interrupts. Watson is full duplex. It listens while it speaks, so you can cut it off, talk over it, or say "yeah" without derailing it.

It also has to decide who it is listening to. Its own voice comes back through the microphone, and so does everyone else in this room. I'll show you how it handles both.

Most voice assistants take turns like a walkie-talkie: one side talks, then the other, and nobody cuts in. Watson is full duplex. It keeps listening while it speaks, so you can interrupt it, talk over it, or say "yeah" and it keeps going.

0:30 · 60 s How it works

The Raspberry Pi is the ears and mouth. It runs voice activity detection, end-of-turn detection, interruption handling and playback. Speech recognition and Watson's voice come from Deepgram, called straight from the Pi, and a fast language model, GLM 5.3 Flash, writes the replies and calls the tools. Everything is one Pipecat pipeline. Big jobs go to an MCP server we built that runs on the Pi: it hands them to a coding agent, pi.dev, locked inside a sandbox folder, and the finished page opens on my Mac the moment it's done.

Two problems took most of the work. First, echo. The speaker is as loud at the mic as I am, and hardware echo cancellation gave us almost nothing. So Watson decides by who is speaking: it compares each interruption against its own voice and against my enrolled voice. Second, my accent. The recognizer used to hear "Watson" as "button" or "Hudson". Part of that was us: the first syllable was being cut off before it reached the recognizer. Now we keep the audio from just before you start speaking, and Deepgram is primed with the word "Watson".

5:15 · 30 s What's left

Three honest limits. The language model still takes about two seconds to start, and sometimes close to five. "Stop" takes up to two seconds, because we confirm the voice before stopping. And echo cancellation on this hardware didn't work, so everything rests on telling voices apart. That's why Watson speaks in a voice deliberately unlike mine.
